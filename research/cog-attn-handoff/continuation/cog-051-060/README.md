# COG-ATTN-051–060 · Grounded acquisition and reuse

The cycle ran experiments for all ten IDs. Three authentic datasets test different capabilities: descriptions of recorded physical actions, turn-by-turn dialogue state changes, and public issue histories. The architecture learned reusable action factors and procedures under identifiable conditions. Its prospective decisions on authentic language missed the joint 99% precision and 70% recall gate. The final autonomous-agent qualification remains open.

The primary controlled panel and issue cohort have frozen protocols. Physical-language and dialogue transfer tests each have a separate protocol committed before fitting. Mechanism repairs that followed a failure are exploratory. The primary panel has three seeds. Seeds on authentic data share observations and do not triple the sample size. ## Three authentic tests

**Recorded kitchen actions.** On 9,668 official EPIC-KITCHENS-100 validation narrations, learning a verb and a noun separately recovered 8,813 exact actions (91.16%), versus 6,937 (71.75%) for a single joint label. On 742 unseen combinations of known constituents, the comparison was 467 versus zero. Both systems trained from scratch on 18,000 official training events, with 7,500 disjoint-video events for calibration. At its calibration-selected threshold, factorization admitted 7,553 actions, of which 7,474 were correct (98.95% precision, 77.31% correct-event recall). The flat control reached 99.22% precision and 57.89% recall. Neither met both targets. [Per-event predictions and inputs](results/kitchen.json)

The annotators' action vocabulary is closely related to the narrations. Masking all literal catalog words derived from training reduced factorized accuracy to 68/9,668 and flat accuracy to 247/9,668. Masking also removes genuinely informative words. A 64-cluster label-free model identified 6,649 current verbs after label alignment and predicted 2,435/9,530 subsequent verbs. Using the first word alone scored 8,298 and 2,475 respectively. A second, participant-disjoint fit tested on 23,568 events: tightening the calibration rule raised precision from 97.54% to 97.72% while reducing recall from 64.04% to 62.73%. The experiments used public narrations and supplied action annotations. They did not consume video pixels. Credit for the source recordings and annotations belongs to Damen et al. and the EPIC-KITCHENS team. This study produced derived labels, models and predictions under CC BY-NC 4.0. Both held slots include unrelated dialogues and turns with no change. The table gives exact updates / attempted updates and recall against all actual updates. Development selected the selective thresholds. Hotel area had been studied in previous cycles. Confidence intervals resample whole test dialogues. **Public issue histories.** The fresh acquisition covered 810 issues from 11 repositories. The strict future-event test contained 29 positives among 1,276 comments in four familiar repositories and 34 among 924 comments in seven new repositories. Adding genuine same-second examples to training raised flat-model average precision from .415 to .541 and from .586 to .718 respectively. Paired issue-bootstrap 95% intervals for the gain were [−.002, .238] and [.027, .248]. The primary separate-expert versus pooled-modular comparison was inconclusive in both groups. The attention-free linear control scored .503 and .725. Additional randomly initialized GRU, Transformer and diagonal state-space controls shared the inputs and a 180-step training budget; their results cannot establish general neural inferiority. [Predictions and estimates](results/summary.json) · [Input-matched controls](results/matched.json)

Staging at the original validation threshold for 80% sensitivity, followed by a new timeline request, found 24/29 and 32/34 real changes, with zero false confirmations. It staged 586 comments and made 418 page requests for 411 distinct issues. Aggregate timed requests, including rate limiting, consumed 500.6 seconds. The median observation wait was 900 seconds. Later evidence came from the provider that defines the behavioral label. The lower one-sided issue-level precision bounds were 87.8% and 90.8%. The result supports a delayed state mirror; it does not establish safe prospective authorization of an action. [Confirmation costs](results/confirmation.json) · [Simulated noisy verification](results/uncertainty.json)

Separating current-state agreement from historical timestamp agreement recovered useful answers when only dates conflicted. On a subsequent October cohort all 175 current answers agreed with the direct endpoint, while 41 disputed times remained unknown. Timeline-only current state also agreed on that cohort. On the main cohort the history index agreed with scan on 7,186 checks, and a separate endpoint returned matching event IDs on 172 selected or sampled issues. GitHub remains the common source. ## When experience made execution cheaper

The first reverse-policy repair admitted a goal after three visits into an eight-entry cache. It sped up hot-goal planning 3.05× and slowed uniform-goal planning to 0.40×. Repeated construction and eviction consumed the saving. The follow-up spent the first 64 turns of each visible 512-turn epoch counting demand, compiled at most eight policies and held that set until the next epoch. On five fresh seeds with randomized measurement order it verified 15,360/15,360 paths. Median speedups including policy construction were 3.29× under concentrated demand and 1.14× under uniform demand. These are planning-only CPU results on 32 states, with free observable epoch notification and a common previously learned graph. [Churning cache](results/policy-repair.json) · [Demand profiling](results/profile-reuse.json)

In the 100-task test the exact store retained 100% of first-acquisition accuracy on every seed. Each task supplied its context identifier and demonstrated all eight inputs. There were four families of finite mappings. Final absolute accuracy was 72.4–74.8% for the upstream BDH model, 23.6–35.5% for the online neural control and 12.8–13.5% for a two-channel readout of native Neuraxon synapses with idle decay. Training schedules and readouts differ. The result identifies a suitable exact-storage workload; it does not rank the architectures generally. [Full retest matrices](results/skills100.json) · [Mechanism controls](results/continual-controls.json)

The strongest 059 control retained the same sufficient statistics as the index. Median online speed ratios against it were 1.17×, 1.03×, 0.89×, 1.05× and 1.12× as context grew. All history records contributed to group means. We used a fixed mean-attention operation, charged real loopback requests and recorded build + 12 × online cost. This measures an aggregate retrieval workload with observable writes. Batched tests record whole-process peak RSS and available CPU threads separately. Full semantic inference on a GPU and cross-host consistency remain unmeasured. [Raw online costs](results/online-cost.json) · [CPU batch measurements](results/parallel-cpu.json)

## Verification and outstanding evidence

Veritas Research Lab OS and TrainLab ran six primary candidate/control jobs plus six clean-directory replays on one host. The three fresh authentic TrainLab runs reproduced their predictions byte-for-byte. A GitHub-hosted Ubuntu runner separately re-induced the panel models and recomputed 45 ranking tables, 960 physical traces and 7,680 scaling answers. Supplemental analyses have individual source and results. This is machine replication under one research operator. No independent research team has replicated the full agent.
