# COG-ATTN-086a/b: supervised option transfer to puzzle search

**Status:** executed on local CPU, 2026-11-21. Research-stage diagnostics, not a passing qualification of general intelligence.

## What is real COG code, and what is new

COG's unchanged `acquire` function was extracted from the COG-066 planning module pinned in the 071–080 research branch. It mines 2–4 step traces under >=8 occurrences and >=3 distinct source families. Puzzle adapters and generic exact A* search were newly implemented for this evaluation. Acquisition consumes successful solution demonstrations generated by a **reference solver**, not sparse rewards or raw sensor streams. This is a test of the *reuse subsystem*, not a learned COG-ATTN chess/Sudoku/maze agent.

## Frozen splits

- **Acquisition:** primary 8101 and 8107: 12 demos/domain × 7 domains = 84 successful plans. Hanoi repeats are not independent roots, so learns zero options.
- **Admission/dev:** primary 8113: 6 tasks/domain × 7 = 42 tasks. Cost gate requires same correctness, lower total primitive evaluations and lower wall time. Zero of seven domain gates accepted.
- **Replication:** 9101, 9107, 9113: 18 unseen test instances/domain × 7 = 126 episodes. All non-Hanoi exact observations were deduplicated between panels.
- Other 4 benchmark conditions (chess Q, chess R, Sudoku 4×4 and 9×9) are not tested in the COG-066 macro transfer.

## Replication results

| Arm | Valid paths | Search states popped | Primitive evaluations | Total search-only CPU elapsed, local diagnostic |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Primitive exact A* | 126/126 | 7,428 | 20,560 | 49.90 ms |
| COG-066 acquired macros | 126/126 | 7,428 | 88,206 | 108.36 ms |
| Count/length-matched shuffled macros | 126/126 | 7,428 | 87,464 | 104.88 ms |
| Dev-calibrated gate | 126/126 | 7,428 | 20,560 | 49.92 ms |

Numbers above are from one local run; wall times vary with host and arm order. The acquired macro arm performed **4.29× as many primitive evaluations** without reducing any popped search states, compared to matched exact search. The shuffled control had similarly high overhead; any perceived timing benefit from just recognizing repeated chunks is unsupported. The seven domains were `maze_7`, `maze_11`, `maze_shift`, `slide_8`, `slide_14`, `hanoi_3`, and `hanoi_5`.

Training/reference solving took approximately 15.3 ms and calibration 52.8 ms on this CPU, **on top of** the replay inference times. Those setup measurements are not counted in the search-only table. The COG option arm is therefore even less economical on total cost under this test design. The guarded arm avoided the extra per-query primitive checks but still paid the one-time profiling cost.

## Why this matters

These options only package already-existing primitive transitions. With an exact admissible state-space search that still checks all available primitive moves, shortcuts can increase work without improving shortest-path solutions. We have *not* shown a general impossibility: a planner with a learned state abstraction, option preconditions, hierarchical subgoals or a different frontier/budget might benefit. This test suggests the next genuine advance requires **selective, state-conditioned skill deployment** rather than unconditional invocation of mined action strings.

## Required follow-up

Train a distinct policy to decide *when* an option has positive value using only observable puzzle state and demonstrated outcomes; compare with development-gated exact search under tight expansions and equal success. Test unseen goal relocation, partial-observation mazes, 3→4/5/6 disc Hanoi transfer with independent configurations, and authenticated chess tactical datasets. Never call full neural COG-ATTN success until the actual controller, its learned memory and closed-loop action policy have been integrated and tested.
