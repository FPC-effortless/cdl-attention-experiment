# COG research results: retrieval, span choice, and state binding

Public results index · 22 November 2026. Aggregates and source pointers; not a release or a claim of complete public reproduction.

**COG-INTERACT-015: finding the paragraph was easier than extracting the answer.** In one 797-pair diagnostic, lexical retrieval found both source paragraphs in 640–641 pairs, but the lexical pipeline answered both questions exactly in 92 pairs. Supplying the annotated source paragraphs raised paired exact match to 100. The observed lexical–neural ranking was small and its paired interval included zero. This is evidence about this reader and this diagnostic, not a general ranking of retrievers. The separate frozen reader follow-up used a restricted panel: an annotation-based sentence mask changed paired exact match from 100 to 149 of 786 pairs. [Public engineering summary](https://github.com/FPC-effortless/veritas/issues/495#issuecomment-7070958805)

**COG-INTERACT-016: choosing a span remained the bottleneck.** On the 797-pair diagnostic, 816 of 1,079 nonexact answers under gold paragraphs were physically disjoint from every annotated span. This does not imply that every disjoint answer was semantically wrong. An annotation-based sentence restriction raised paired exact match from 100 to 151 of 797. This result has a different denominator from the 015 follow-up above. With the two annotated alternatives supplied, the frozen reader strictly preferred the right alternative for both questions in 424 of 790 eligible pairs. In 329 of those pairs, unrestricted extraction failed at least one question. These are oracle diagnostics, not label-free scores or proof of assigning changing relations correctly. A tested label-free decoder raised token-level F1 but reduced paired exact answers from 92 to 85. Lean checks covered the exported finite scoring and accounting, not the entire neural implementation. [Public council summary](https://github.com/FPC-effortless/veritas/issues/495#issuecomment-7071448850)

**COG-BIND-SYN-001: direct write supervision helped in a controlled synthetic task.** Five matched seeds compared four objectives on a 157-parameter model. Both write-supervised arms solved all 480 synthetic evaluation families in every seed; read-only supervision and no auxiliary supervision each averaged 28% complete families. An exact deterministic control with the same inputs also solved all 480. The model was given typed equality and rank inputs. This establishes a bounded objective effect under those inputs, not an advantage over deterministic rules or evidence of raw-language binding, long-horizon retention, or lower inference cost. This file preserves the public aggregate; the full evidence bundle is not included.

**COG-LM-004: stop rather than scale this memory route.** None of the 13 evaluated configurations passed the defined strict binding outcomes. The best reported lookup-enabled validation loss for the relevant intervention arms was 4.143 versus 3.936 for the parent, exceeding the allowed +0.10. The source-and-aggregate audit supported the STOP decision; it was not an independent rerun of the original models. Zero jointly eligible two-orientation groups does not establish zero single-orientation executions. [Public audit checkpoint](https://github.com/FPC-effortless/veritas/issues/482#issuecomment-7068903421)

**COG-PULSE-006: direct current-event access improved writes, not reliable answers.** Ten CPU fits compared two equal-size variants across five matched seeds on 480 synthetic paired families. Address accuracy rose from 61.1% to 69.6%; joint address, commit and value accuracy rose from 38.8% to 43.4%. Strict paired answers were 8/2,400 versus 6/2,400, a +0.08 percentage point difference with a paired 95% interval of −0.21 to +0.38. Neither arm solved a long-history pair (0/600 each). The prescribed promotion rule failed. Same-host restore reproduced the primary predictions; cross-machine replay is not established. These results motivate a stable-reader diagnostic, not an assumption that it will fix the model.
