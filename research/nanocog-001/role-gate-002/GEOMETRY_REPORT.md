# ROLE-GATE-002: the probability-matched sham did not clear coverage

This capture-only stage tested whether equal **probability-space** changes
could support a fair directional comparison at the final local gate.
There was no intervention, answer scoring, or training. The two same
frozen checkpoints and 32 exposed ROLE-GATE-001 families were reused
as a named mechanistic diagnostic, not a holdout.

An individual leg qualified if the registered non-collinear permuted
direction allowed a common ideal L2 perturbation of at least 0.10.
A history qualified only if both query legs did. The locked screen
required **103/128 histories and 26/32 families** with at least three
qualified histories per family, separately on both checkpoints.

| Seed | Eligible legs / 256 | Eligible histories / 128 | Eligible families / 32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1171 strong | 143 | 15 | 0 |
| 1371 weak | 154 | 26 | 5 |

All 128 histories in both checkpoints had an uncapped natural donor
distance of at least 0.10. Each history had at least one eligible leg;
most did not have two. The limited support comes from the interaction
of the registered angular sham and probability support with the
starting state, not from an absent natural query shift. This does
**not** prove continuous infeasibility, a null gate effect, or a
repair. No equal-dose swap ran, and the original probe's dose-size
caveat remains.

The diagnostic decisively removes this registered sham comparison as
an adequately supported next test. An alternative scientific question
is whether any predeclared valid gate state can recover failed answers
while all other paths stay fixed. A blind finite enumeration can test
that reachability without a dose-comparison claim. If selected
retrospectively using the answer, a rescue is explicitly oracle
diagnostic only; it does not demonstrate a controller or qualification.
